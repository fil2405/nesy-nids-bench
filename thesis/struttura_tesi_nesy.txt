// Titolo: Benchmarking Neurosymbolic Approaches for Network Traffic Analysis

0.2  PROBLEM STATEMENT
     Neurosymbolic AI is repeatedly claimed to help in network security (better generalization,
     better handling of rare attacks, explainable alerts), but the literature evaluates it with
     heterogeneous protocols, mostly on accuracy alone, on a single dataset, without cost or
     latency figures, and almost never under deployment constraints. There is no shared answer
     to the practical question a SOC engineer asks: "under which conditions is a neurosymbolic
     detector worth its extra complexity?"

0.3  RESEARCH QUESTIONS
     RQ1  Detection quality. Does injecting domain knowledge through LTN improve detection
          over an identical purely neural model, and on which classes / attack families?
     RQ2  Operating regime. Under which conditions does the NeSy advantage appear and
          disappear? (amount of labelled data, class imbalance, unseen attacks, cross-dataset
          shift, quality of the encoded knowledge)
     RQ3  Cost. What is the computational price of the NeSy approach in training and in
          inference, in batch and in streaming operation?
     RQ4  Usability. What does the approach cost in engineering effort, and what does it give
          back in terms of interpretable and auditable alerts?
     RQ5  Methodology. What does a fair, reproducible benchmark for ML vs NeSy network traffic
          analysis have to look like?

0.4  CLAIMED CONTRIBUTIONS (state them explicitly in Chapter 1 and revisit in Chapter 11)
     C1  A reproducible benchmark suite for ML vs NeSy network traffic analysis covering three
         axes - detection quality, computational performance, operational usability - in both
         batch and real-time settings.
     C2  A systematic empirical comparison of an LTN-based detector against matched ML/DL
         baselines across multiple datasets and multiple stress scenarios (scarce labels,
         imbalance, unseen attacks, cross-dataset transfer, degraded knowledge).
     C3  A characterisation of the operating regime in which neurosymbolic integration pays off,
         including the negative results (where it does not).
     C4  A streaming reference pipeline that runs an LTN-trained detector on live traffic, with
         end-to-end latency and throughput measurements.
     C5  Two evaluation metrics that the mainstream NIDS literature does not report and that are
         natural for NeSy systems: axiom-level satisfaction as an explanation signal, and
         domain-rule violation rate of the predictions.

0.5  OUT OF SCOPE
     - Encrypted-traffic payload inspection and TLS fingerprinting.
     - Adversarial machine learning beyond the simple evasion perturbations of E10.
     - Distributed / multi-node training; single-workstation setting only.
     - Production-grade SOC integration (SIEM, ticketing, response).
